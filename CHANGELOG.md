# Changelog

## 0.1.0

First release.

- **The Marketplace window**, opened from the Actors tab: markets with a picture, description and level, each holding the PF2e merchants in its folder (and any added by dragging them onto it); shop cards; each shop's stock by group (weapons, armor and shields, consumables, runes, gear, treasure) with level, rarity, stock and price, and descriptions on a click. Shops sell identified goods: an item the merchant has unidentified shows as the real item.
- **Visible and Open** on each market: players can browse a visible market and trade in an open one. A shop can be hidden from players.
- **Buying**: a cart across a market's open shops, bought for one of your characters or the party stash. The GM's client makes every trade, so a GM must be logged in. It checks the stock, the price and the buyer's money first, takes the coins (making change), puts the items in the buyer's inventory, identified and stacked, and pays the shop. If anything changed, nothing happens and the player is told why.
- **Selling**: a shop's Sell tab lists what the character carries with the shop's offer (half an item's price and full value for treasure by default, set per shop). Unidentified items have to be identified first. What a shop buys goes into its stock, and it pays from its purse, the coins on its merchant sheet, unless the GM gives it an unlimited purse.
- **Purses**: the New shop button makes a merchant at the market's level with a full purse: one level's party treasure for its level (GM Core's table; 100 gp below level 1). Fill purse tops any shop up to that, and a world setting scales it.
- **Shopkeepers**: drag an NPC onto a shop. Their attitude changes what players pay when buying, never what the shop pays them (Friendly 10% off, Helpful 20% off, Unfriendly 20% more by default, all settings; a hostile shopkeeper won't trade unless that setting is off). With PF2e NPC Attitudes, it's the attitude the party sees, toward each character where set; if the party is shown a different attitude from the true one, prices are normal. Without it, a setting on the shop. A shop never pays a character more for something than it would charge them for it.
- **The trade log**, shown only to the GM: every purchase and sale, who made it, the shop and the money.
